Fiber & Grain Hemp Is a Crop

A short public statement for everyone who grows, builds with, sells or cares about hemp for fiber, hurd and grain. Read it, add your name, and tell your lawmakers.

The statement

Hemp grown for fiber, hurd and grain is a farm crop, like corn, soybeans and cotton. Since November 2025, federal law has defined “industrial hemp” as its own category, separate from cannabinoid products.[1] But the farm safety net hasn’t caught up. Hemp is not a “covered commodity” or a marketing-loan crop under Title I of farm law,[2][3] and its main crop insurance policy is still a pilot.[4]

We, the undersigned, ask Congress, USDA, the State of California and local governments to:

Keep the House Farm Bill’s industrial hemp provisions

Section 10005 of H.R. 7567 passed the House 224–200 on April 30, 2026. It lets growers designate a crop as industrial hemp and allows lighter testing for it, such as visual inspection, performance-based sampling or certified seed.[5][6][7] The Senate Agriculture Committee’s bill, advanced 12–11 on September 16, 2026, has no hemp language.[8][9] Keep Sec. 10005 in the final Farm Bill.

Give hemp commodity-program access like corn, soy and cotton

Title I programs (reference prices, ARC/PLC and marketing loans) cover crops listed in the law, and hemp isn’t one of them.[2][3] Adding hemp fiber takes an act of Congress, the way seed cotton was added in 2018.[20] For hemp grain, ask USDA to study whether it can be designated an “other oilseed,” which the law allows the Secretary to do.[2] The National Industrial Hemp Council has asked for hemp to be included in USDA subsidy programs.[12]

Make hemp crop insurance permanent

Today hemp growers rely on a pilot Actual Production History policy in select counties, plus Whole-Farm Revenue Protection and NAP.[4][10] Move hemp to a permanent Risk Management Agency policy. The American Farm Bureau Federation’s 2026 policy supports a permanent, hemp-specific policy.[11]

Fund hemp processing

A crop only works if someone can process it. Fund grants and loan guarantees for fiber decortication, grain processing and hemp building-material plants. Existing programs like Value-Added Producer Grants and Business & Industry loan guarantees are a start, but hemp needs dedicated support.[13][14]

Adopt hempcrete codes in California and Los Angeles

IRC Appendix BL (hemp-lime) is printed in the 2025 California Residential Code but isn’t in force for most homes.[15][16] Adopt it statewide in the next code cycle. A 2025 state law (AB 130) limits new local building standards for homes until June 1, 2031, so ask cities, starting with Los Angeles, to approve hempcrete under alternative-materials rules and adopt BL wherever they can.[17] See the California tracker →

Contact your lawmakers

Tell the Agriculture Committees

The Farm Bill is written in the House and Senate Agriculture Committees. If one of your members sits on either committee, your message counts even more.[18][19] Keep it short: who you are, where you live, and one ask from this page.
