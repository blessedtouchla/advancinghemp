United States

Landmarks on the way

  • 2010 · Asheville, North CarolinaThe Martin-Korp house, designed and built by Push Design, is often called the first American hempcrete house. It was built with imported materials.[6][7]
  • 2014 · Rolling Hills, L.A. CountyA permitted hempcrete home extension, supervised by TerraBuilt Construction. It was the first California hemp build I found on the map.[8]
  • 2017 · Denver, ColoradoDenver permitted its first hempcrete structure, a small post-and-beam workshop.[6]
  • 2022–2024 · The codeRB316-22 was approved and published as IRC Appendix BL. More on codes →[6]
  • 2026 · Los AngelesHemp Homes announced L.A.’s first permitted hempcrete ADU.[9]

Europe

Where it all started

Europe had a head start while growing hemp was still illegal in the U.S.[6]

  • France, 1986: the first modern hemp-lime infill, at Nogent-sur-Seine. By 2012 it was on the list of techniques French insurers accept.[6]
  • England, ~2001: two hemp-lime homes were built alongside sixteen conventional ones at Haverhill, Suffolk, so the Building Research Establishment could compare them.[6]
  • England, 2006: the Adnams brewery distribution centre in Suffolk used hemp-lime walls at commercial scale.[6]
  • Cambridgeshire, 2019: Flat House at Margent Farm by Practice Architecture: prefabricated panels filled with hemp grown on the farm, raised into place in two days.[10]

Beyond hemp

Earth building & CalEarth

I’m also inspired by earth architecture. CalEarth, the institute started by architect Nader Khalili in Hesperia, California, researches and teaches Superadobe, a way of building with long earth-filled bags. It’s another route to low-impact homes right here in Southern California.[11]
