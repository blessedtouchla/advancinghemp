California hemp building code tracker

Where hempcrete stands in California

The status of IRC Appendix BL (hemp-lime) in California and its cities, with sources. Last checked: Sep 25, 2026.

The state: printed, not adopted for homes

Appendix BL is printed in the 2025 California Residential Code.[1] But California’s code says appendix provisions don’t apply unless a state agency adopts them or a local enforcing agency adopts them.[3] The Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD), which sets the residential rules for most homes, proposed not to adopt Appendix BL in the 2024 triennial cycle.[4] USHBA reports that California declined to adopt it, so it’s printed as non-mandatory.[5]

One detail worth knowing: the code’s adoption table for Appendix BL marks the State Fire Marshal as adopting the appendix with amendments to Section BL105 (fire resistance).[2] The State Fire Marshal’s adoptions apply to the building types under its authority, not automatically to every house or ADU, so ask your building department what applies to your project. USHBA has asked for the primary document on a reported State Fire Marshal recognition of hemp-lime.[5]

Cities and counties

Cities and counties normally adopt voluntary appendices like this one by local ordinance filed with the California Building Standards Commission.[6] But a 2025 state law, AB 130, put a moratorium on new state and local building standards for homes from October 1, 2025 to June 1, 2031, with narrow exceptions.[7] So a California city may not be able to newly adopt Appendix BL for homes before 2031 unless an exception applies. Projects can still be approved under the code’s alternative materials, design and methods provision. USHBA says it did this work before Appendix BL existed and still does most of it that way.[5]

In force (per USHBA, Aug 2026)

Three places in the U.S.

USHBA counts three jurisdictions where Appendix BL is actually law: Austin, Texas, and Maricopa County and the City of Surprise in Arizona. None are in California.[5]

Sources don’t fully agree: HempBuild Magazine reported in September 2026 that the City of Phoenix adopted BL effective August 1, 2025, and that Maricopa County has not.[11] I’m checking.

Los Angeles context

Permitted hempcrete projects

In May 2026, Hemp Homes announced that Los Angeles permitted a hempcrete ADU.[8] L.A. County permitted a hempcrete home extension in Rolling Hills in 2014 as insulating infill.[9] More on the Codes & Permits page.

What to ask

Questions for your city or county

Bring these to your building department, planning counter or city council office. Put them in writing and keep the answers.

  1. Has our jurisdiction adopted Appendix BL of the California Residential Code? If not, why not?[3]
  2. Given AB 130, can the city adopt BL now, or does it have to wait for the 2031 cycle?[7]
  3. Until then, what do you need to approve hemp-lime under the alternative materials, design and methods provision?[5]
  4. Which lab tests, engineering and fire information will you accept? (The permit checklist has more.)
  5. Will the city support adopting BL statewide in the next California code cycle?
