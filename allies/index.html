Hemp Organizations

Groups working for fiber & grain hemp

Organizations publicly working on hemp as a farm crop and a building material: farm policy, building codes, feed and markets. Descriptions are my short summaries of their own public pages, checked Sep 25, 2026.

Being listed here doesn’t mean a group partners with or endorses Advancing Hemp, or that I endorse everything a group does. Positions change, so follow the source links. Is your group missing? Email us.

Farm and policy groups

Trade association · national

National Industrial Hemp Council of America (NIHC)

What they push: “Fit for Purpose” rules that license and test hemp by end use, with lighter reporting for fiber and grain. In 2023 it asked the Senate to designate hemp as both a specialty and a commodity crop and include it in USDA subsidy programs. Source

Visit nihcoa.com →

Trade association · national

National Hemp Association (NHA)

What they push: Has worked only on fiber and grain since March 2025. Leads the “Goodness of Hemp Act” framework (not introduced in Congress as of Sep 2026), which would cut red tape for industrial hemp farmers and grow feed markets. The framework also sets rules for cannabinoid products. Source

Visit nationalhempassociation.org →

Nonprofit · national

Hemp Education & Marketing Initiatives (HEMI)

What they push: Runs The Goodness of Hemp campaign for fiber and grain products, including building materials. Its HEMI Working Group (with NHA, the Hemp Feed Coalition and USHBA) meets monthly and is open to fiber and grain nonprofits and associations. Source

Visit hempinitiatives.org →

Nonprofit · national

Hemp Feed Coalition

What they push: Works to get hemp grain by-products approved as animal feed. Hemp seed meal for laying hens was published in the AAFCO Official Publication in August 2025. Source

Visit hempfeedcoalition.org →

General farm organization · national

American Farm Bureau Federation

What they push: Its 2026 policy book supports regulating hemp like other farm commodities, a permanent hemp crop insurance policy, and separating grain and fiber hemp from cannabinoid hemp in law. Policy starts with county and state Farm Bureaus. Source

Visit www.fb.org →

Conference · Denver

Industrial Hemp International (IHI)

What they push: Convenes the industry around separate lanes for fiber, grain and floral hemp, and frames hemp as a commodity crop alongside corn, soy and cotton. Source

Visit industrialhempinternational.com →

Hemp building groups

Trade association · national

U.S. Hemp Building Association (USHBA)

What they push: Pushes adoption of IRC Appendix BL (hemp-lime) and fire-rated hempcrete assemblies for the 2027 IRC, and tracks where BL is actually in force. It has asked for California permit stories. Source

Visit www.ushempbuilding.org →

Nonprofit · national

Hemp Building Institute (HBI)

What they push: Research, education and affordable housing with hemp building materials. Source

Visit www.hempbuildinginstitute.org →

Nonprofit · international

International Hemp Building Association (IHBA)

What they push: Promotes hemp construction materials and runs the International Hemp Building Symposium (the 13th was held in Minnesota in October 2025). Source

Visit internationalhempbuilding.org →

Trade media · international

HempBuild Magazine

What they push: Reports on hemp building projects and code wins, and publishes the Hemp Building Directory 2026. Source

Visit www.hempbuildmag.com →

Regional and California

Fiber and grain processor · Montana

IND HEMP

What they push: Processes hemp fiber and grain and contracts growers in the Northwest. Hosts the free “Hemp in the Built Environment” symposium in Belgrade, Montana on Oct 8, 2026. Source

Visit indhemp.com →

State advisory board · California

CDFA Industrial Hemp Advisory Board

What they push: Not an advocacy group: it advises the California Department of Food and Agriculture on hemp rules and budget. Meetings are public and take comments of up to 2 minutes per item. Source

Visit www.cdfa.ca.gov/plant/industrialhemp →

International models

Trade association · EU

European Industrial Hemp Association (EIHA)

What they push: For the EU’s post-2028 farm policy, it welcomes recognition of the whole hemp plant as an agricultural raw material and asks for a 1% THC limit. Source

Visit eiha.org →

Trade association · Canada

Canadian Hemp Trade Alliance (CHTA)

What they push: Its 2026 submission to Health Canada asks to treat hemp as an agricultural crop, with a 1% THC limit and fewer licences and tests. Source

Visit www.hemptrade.ca →

Industry cluster · France

Pôle Européen du Chanvre / World Hemp Forum

What they push: An industrial hemp cluster in Troyes. It hosts the World Hemp Forum (Nov 24–26, 2026), with a public-policy panel and a hemp buildings tour. Source

Visit www.pole-europeen-chanvre.eu →

California

I couldn’t find an active statewide trade group in California focused on fiber and grain hemp. If you know of one, or want to help start one, email us.
