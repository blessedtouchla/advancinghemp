Suppliers & Getting Started

From curious to on-site

I’ve talked with suppliers, ordered samples and joined a workshop. Here’s the path I’d follow, the companies I’ve dealt with, and an honest look at cost.

Getting started, step by step

Learn the material

Read Hempcrete 101, then get your hands dirty: a workshop or a sample kit teaches more than any video.

Check your code first

Ask your building department whether Appendix BL is adopted, or what an alternative-materials request needs. Checklist →

Find experienced people

Use the builder maps and directories to find designers, engineers and installers near you.

Source materials

Hurd, binder, blocks, batts: ask where each one is grown and made. I want to see more U.S.-grown hurd in U.S. buildings.

Plan for drying & plaster

Hemp-lime needs vapor-open forms, finishes and time to dry. Build that into the schedule.[8]

Document & share

Photograph every step and share what your building department asked for. That’s how the next person gets approved faster.

Suppliers I’ve worked with or follow

Listing isn’t an endorsement, and no one paid to be here. Ask each company for current specs, lead times and pricing.

Blocks

Hemp Block USA

Precast hemp-lime blocks. In 2022 I talked with them about a small building project. My big question then, and still: where are the blocks and the hurd produced? I want to build with U.S. hemp.[1]

Insulation batts

Hempitecture HempWool

Hemp-fiber batt insulation for framed walls, floors and ceilings. In July I shared a post from Tiny Hemp Homes about ordering 7.5″ HempWool batts for a Santa Barbara project.[2]

Raw materials & samples

Bulk Hemp Warehouse

Bulk hemp fibers, samples and fabrics. In 2024 I ordered building samples from them (hemp rebar, hemp fiber board and a small hempcrete DIY kit) to see and feel the materials myself.[3]

Honest costs

Why hemp can cost more, for now

On June 13 I shared a post called “The cost of building with hemp: understanding the trade-offs.” These are the trade-offs it walked through, with sources where I have them. I’m not publishing prices: they change fast and depend on your project, so get current quotes.

  • Upfront cost: materials and specialized labor can cost more than conventional walls. In the UK’s Haverhill trial (2001), researchers estimated the hemp-lime homes cost about 10% more, mostly because of labor.[6]
  • Processing: U.S. hemp processing is still being built out. USHBA puts it bluntly: the U.S. got a code pathway before it got a supply chain.[6]
  • Transport: early American hempcrete walls used imported hurd and binder, and shipping bulky material a long way adds cost.[6]
  • Training: vapor-open detailing and good mixing take skill, so you’re paying for experienced installers or your own learning curve.[8]
  • Market maturity: financing and lending are still catching up with the code.[7]
  • Long-term side of the ledger: in the same Haverhill trial, the hemp-lime homes used less energy once people lived in them, even though they scored worse on paper.[6]
